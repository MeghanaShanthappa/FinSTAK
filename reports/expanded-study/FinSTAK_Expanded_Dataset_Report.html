FinSTAK: the expanded-data study

100 stocks • 2014–2023 • 27,030 source-checked event records | Research report • 22 November 2026

FinSTAK forecasts the next trading session’s open, high, low and close (OHLC) by combining each stock’s recent market history with source-linked corporate events and relationships between companies. This report documents the larger dataset, the completed architecture experiments, and the independent checks performed for this report.

Main finding. The larger experiment is reproducible and shows a small exploratory reduction in price error from event inputs. It does not establish better direction prediction than a simple majority baseline. Adding the full set of new architecture components worsened price error.

1. What dataset did we add?

A larger research panel, rather than a new off-the-shelf labelled dataset

The earlier FinSTAK experiment used 16 stocks, 2016–2019 observations and 365 eligible events. The expanded study uses a custom 100-company subset covering ten calendar years, assembled from public news, price and disclosure sources. The requested 25,000–50,000 event-record target was reached with 27,030 retained records.

2. Verification, timing and coverage

What “source-checked” means in this experiment

News records are collected using deterministic asserted-event rules applied to quoted headlines or article sentences. A retained assertion must have company-name evidence, a supported category rule, a traceable source, and a preserved text span. Speculative wording is excluded. Duplicate and near-duplicate rules are applied, and SEC observations that plausibly repeat a reporting episode are excluded conservatively.

Verification boundary. These are automated source-evidence checks. No records have human semantic certification, and the process does not independently establish the truth of reported news or perfect economic-event deduplication. All 27,030 exported confidence fields are null; this run does not evaluate calibrated confidence-weighted event edges.

Information availability and graph lifetime

The recorded decision cutoff is 23:59:59 UTC on each trading session. News becomes eligible two calendar days after its reported publication timestamp; this is a conservative assumption, not a verified historical arrival time. SEC availability follows the later filing/UTC acceptance date. Category age limits retain earnings for up to 90 sessions, litigation for up to 500, and other categories for up to 15, subject to any explicit validity boundary. “No decay” disables learned soft attenuation; it does not disable eligibility or these hard age limits.

3. How FinSTAK works

Local market history → event disturbance → related companies → next-session candle

Market-history branch

Each stock supplies 19 causal price, volume and technical features. The first four OHLC channels share trailing 90-session price normalization; log-volume is normalized separately. The remaining feature scalers are fitted only on the training period. A learned company embedding and 32-unit GRU encode 30 observed sessions per stock. The combined recipe uses 20 sessions and two recurrent layers.

Event and relationship branch

Frozen text vectors and category embeddings enter four-head typed attention, queried by the local stock state. A learned null option lets the model suppress event messages. Events enter explicitly named companies, after which one hop transports the event shock to up to three positively correlated peers. Correlations use 60 observed returns and a 0.2 minimum weight. Sector variants replace this peer route with company–sector–company disturbance aggregation, excluding each recipient’s own contribution.

Candle and auxiliary heads

The model predicts open and close in log-price coordinates relative to the observed decision-day close. Positive upper and lower excursions construct high and low around those bounds, enforcing valid OHLC order. Experimental heads add volume or direction targets, detached wick features, or a separate dispersion stream. These targets are next-session prices and volume; the expanded study is not a market-adjusted stock-direction classifier.

Implementation boundary: this is a project-specific recurrent encoder and typed-attention model. It is not a full HGT reproduction. Event extraction in this scale experiment is rule-based, and event attention uses unit source weights rather than calibrated event-confidence values. Evidence: [E5].

What the combined recipe changes

The combined condition uses a 20-session two-layer GRU, FinBERT text vectors, hybrid decay, sector routing, a primary-discloser filter, isolated wick gradients, a volume head, an auxiliary direction head and PCGrad. It is a joint hypothesis test. Its result cannot isolate which component or interaction causes a change in performance.

Holdout status. These are chronological historical tests, but the years were already examined during research. The fresh November run reproduces the same panel, recipes and test folds; it is not new independent out-of-sample evidence.

Shared OHLCV reaches 21.78% volume MAPE, compared with 27.25% for previous-volume persistence: about 20.05% lower relative MAPE. Among the tested neural conditions, naive PCGrad has lower volume MSE than shared OHLCV, but slightly higher MAPE (22.10% versus 21.78%). Retransformation and task interference

Gaussian residual correction and empirical smearing are fitted only on the preceding validation year, separately by stock. For shared OHLCV, these increase pooled MAPE to 24.70% and 25.03%, respectively. Some MSE comparisons favor different corrections. No retransformation is uniformly best, and PCGrad does not guarantee unbiased conditional means or remove every task conflict.

Adding volume tasks does not establish improved OHLC forecasting. Shared OHLCV has 0.966195% price error; PCGrad has 0.966139%; both exceed no-decay FinSTAK’s 0.964184%. The exploratory PCGrad-versus-shared OHLC interval includes zero. Evidence: [E2], [E6].

10. What remains unresolved?

Limits that matter for interpreting the expanded results

AreaCurrent limitationConsequence
Event qualityAutomated evidence checks; no human labels; imperfect economic-event deduplication.Source attribution is auditable, but semantic precision and distinct-event count remain unmeasured.
Event confidenceAll exported confidence values are null; attention uses unit event-source weights.The confidence-weighted graph idea has not been validated by this run.
Historical timingAssumed news delay, static groups and retrospectively adjusted prices.The panel is not a complete reconstruction of information available to a historical trader.
UniverseManually curated candidate list and full observed panel; pre-2014 eligibility and sector quotas.Pre-period eligibility reduces one selection channel but does not eliminate survivorship or selection bias.
EvaluationThree previously examined annual tests, many configurations and uncorrected intervals.The evidence is exploratory; a new untouched test is needed for a stronger claim.
Text encodersFrozen pretrained embeddings with unknown training-period overlap.Chronological stock splits do not eliminate foundation-model pretraining overlap.
ExplainabilitySources and graph routes are traceable, but this expanded run has no new explanation-faithfulness study.Attention weights or graph paths alone do not establish a causal explanation for a forecast.
Practical usefulnessNo transaction-cost strategy test or prospective deployment.Price or volume accuracy does not establish profitability.

Recommended next experiments, in order

1. Isolate scaling. On matched test dates and the same focal stocks, compare training universes and event budgets while holding architecture, eligibility, normalization and checkpoint selection fixed. This separates gains from extra training stocks, longer history and more events.

2. Measure event quality. Create an independent annotation set spanning source, category, company and year. Audit entity links, semantic event labels and duplicated reporting episodes before introducing calibrated confidence values.

3. Test event content. Add matched historical content-shuffle and timestamp controls, with a fixed primary comparison and uncertainty method. Test whether any small gain depends on meaningful event content rather than event availability or category frequency.

4. Freeze and evaluate forward. Reserve a genuinely untouched later period, report volatility-normalized price errors and directional baselines, and evaluate any trading objective separately. Historical supply-chain edges and analyst-consensus surprises require real point-in-time data; they are not implemented here.

Metric definitions

Normalized OHLC MAE (%) = 100 × mean over test stock-dates and four price fields of |predicted next-session price − observed next-session price| / observed decision-day close. Annual results are pooled by their actual stock-date counts, rather than averaging years equally.

Close direction accuracy compares the signs of predicted and observed next-close changes relative to the observed decision-day close. The majority benchmark is chosen using training data. Within-1% field rate counts individual fields whose absolute error is at most 1% of the observed decision-day close. Whole-candle rate requires all four fields to satisfy the tolerance.

Volume MAPE averages absolute percentage error over positive observed volumes. Volume MSE averages squared errors in shares; large-volume stocks and outliers can dominate this metric. Price checkpoints are still selected by validation OHLC MAE, even for volume-capable configurations.

The native TimesFM baseline is not rerun in the November reproduction. The retained older expanded-study package reports 1.219086% error for its frozen native TimesFM comparator. That archived benchmark is excluded from the fresh-run ranking above, which covers the 153 FinSTAK-family and control fits. Evidence: [E1], [E2], [E8].
